## Executive summary

This is a grounded BUILD: a retrieval-augmented policy assistant that answers employee HR/IT policy questions with citations back to the source document. The spine of the build is the acceptance bar — ≥90% of answers on a 100-question test set rated correct-with-citation by HR reviewers, with zero tolerance for fabricated policy claims. Because the task shape is lookup, the engineering effort concentrates on ingestion quality, chunking and metadata, retrieval precision, and refusal behaviour when the corpus does not contain an answer — not on model sophistication. Data readiness is the weakest input (scored 3), so the plan front-loads a hands-on corpus audit before any modelling work, and treats sensitive-content exclusion (salary bands, individual HR records) as an ingestion-time filter rather than a prompt-time hope. Delivery is scoped to one builder over roughly one quarter (the engagement states 8 weeks; durations below are estimates), shipping through shadow mode and sampled review before general availability, with a pre-committed rollback trigger.

**Architecture pattern:** Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)

## Plan

### Architecture — RAG grounded in the policy corpus

**Pattern (from grounding): Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).** The task is answering questions from an existing document corpus, so answers must be assembled from retrieved passages rather than model memory. This is what makes the citation requirement satisfiable and keeps the system current as handbooks change, without retraining.

**Components (capabilities, not products):**
1. **Connectors** to the document store holding policy PDFs and handbook pages, plus a separate, clearly-labelled path for FAQ threads from the chat tool. Chat threads are lower-authority content and must be tagged as such.
2. **Ingestion & normalisation** — PDF/HTML to text, layout-aware where tables and clause numbering matter, boilerplate stripping (headers, footers, revision blocks).
3. **Chunking with policy-aware boundaries** — chunk on headings/clauses rather than fixed character windows, so a retrieved passage is a self-contained rule an HR reviewer would accept as a citation.
4. **Metadata layer** — document title, owning team, effective date, version, jurisdiction/entity if policies differ, sensitivity label, and authority tier (official policy vs. FAQ thread). Retrieval filters on these.
5. **A managed vector index plus lexical retrieval** — hybrid retrieval matters here because policy questions often hinge on exact terms ("bereavement", "carryover", specific form numbers) that dense embeddings blur.
6. **Answer composition** with mandatory citation: every assertion maps to a retrieved chunk; the response surfaces document name, section and effective date.
7. **Abstention path** — if retrieval confidence or coverage is insufficient, the system says it cannot find the policy and routes the user to HR/IT rather than composing an answer. Given the zero-fabrication clause, abstention is a first-class success state, not a failure.
8. **Feedback capture** — thumbs/flag plus free text on every answer, written to the evaluation store.

**Deliberately out of scope for the pilot:** transactional actions (submitting leave, opening tickets), personalised answers based on an individual's HR record, and multi-turn negotiation of edge cases. Each of these changes the risk profile and should be re-evaluated as its own case.

**Interactive latency** is the stated requirement; achieve it by keeping retrieval to a single hybrid pass with a reranking step and streaming the response. No specific latency figure is committed here — a target should be set from measured baselines in Phase 2.

### Data pipeline — the weakest link, handled first

Data readiness scored 3 out of 5, the lowest input in the evaluation. Treat that as the primary technical risk.

**Corpus audit (before any indexing):**
- Enumerate every policy document in the store: owner, last-updated date, whether it is the authoritative version. Expect duplicates, superseded drafts and "final_v3_FINAL" collisions — these are the single most common cause of confidently-cited wrong answers.
- Manually read a sample across document types. Note scanned/image-only PDFs (need OCR or exclusion), documents where meaning lives in tables, and documents that contradict each other.
- Decide the authority ruling for conflicts: which document wins, and who adjudicates. Without this, retrieval will surface both and the answer will be wrong for half of readers.

**Sensitive-content exclusion (compliance requirement):**
- Salary bands and individual HR records must never enter the index. Implement as an allowlist of ingested locations plus a sensitivity classifier/pattern screen at ingestion, with a manual review of anything flagged. Excluding at ingestion is far stronger than instructing the model at query time.
- Log every exclusion decision so HR can audit what the assistant can and cannot see.

**Chat-thread handling:** FAQ threads are useful for phrasing real questions but are not policy. Two defensible options: (a) exclude from the answer corpus and mine them only to build the golden question set, or (b) ingest with a distinct authority tier that can never be the sole citation. Recommend (a) for the pilot — it removes a whole class of fabrication risk at near-zero cost to coverage.

**Refresh:** freshness is periodic, so a scheduled incremental re-ingest keyed to source modification timestamps is sufficient — no streaming needed. Deletions and supersessions must propagate: a removed policy must disappear from the index, or the assistant will cite a rule that no longer exists. Include effective-date metadata in every answer so a stale citation is visible to the reader.

**Repeatability:** the prep pipeline is code, versioned, re-runnable end-to-end. No manual conversion steps that only the builder knows how to repeat.

### Evaluation — the acceptance bar as pass/fail gate

**The bar (verbatim):** ≥90% of answers on a 100-question test set rated correct-with-citation by HR reviewers; zero fabricated policy claims tolerated. Everything below ladders to this.

**Golden set construction (100 items):** draw from real question history — repeat questions to HR/IT and the FAQ threads are the best source of authentic phrasing. Compose deliberately:
- routine, high-frequency questions;
- questions whose answer depends on entity/jurisdiction/date (tests metadata filtering);
- questions answered by tables or nested clauses;
- questions where policies conflict or a policy was recently updated;
- **unanswerable questions** — not covered by the corpus, or deliberately touching excluded sensitive content. Correct behaviour is abstention plus referral. These items police the zero-fabrication clause.
HR owns the reference answers and citations. Freeze the set; changes are versioned.

**Scoring rubric.** Each answer is scored on: (1) factual correctness against the cited source; (2) citation present, specific and actually supporting the claim; (3) no unsupported assertions; (4) appropriate abstention. Any unsupported policy assertion is a fabrication and is counted separately — the fabrication count is a hard gate independent of the 90% figure, so a run can hit 90% correct and still fail.

**LLM-as-judge:** may be used to scale scoring, but only after measuring judge-vs-human agreement on a subsample. If agreement is not demonstrably high, humans score. Judge–human agreement is an empirical question, not an assumption.

**Failure taxonomy.** Log every miss as retrieval miss (right answer not retrieved), synthesis error (retrieved correctly, answered wrongly), citation error (right answer, wrong or vague pointer), stale-source error, or over-refusal. Each has a different fix — chunking/metadata, prompt/reranking, output format, refresh cadence, threshold tuning. Reporting a single accuracy number without this breakdown wastes the run.

**Red-teaming** targets the named risk: fabricated answers presented with confident citations. Probe with questions that sound like policy but aren't covered, leading questions that presuppose a false rule, and requests for salary/individual-record data. Findings become permanent golden-set items.

**Live measurement** continues after launch: sampled review of production answers scored on the same rubric, plus user flags triaged weekly.

### Governance, oversight and rollback

**Oversight model (from grounding): spot-check.** Start with sampled human review calibrated to the acceptance bar, and reduce sampling only as measured accuracy stabilises on live traffic. Sampling rate reductions are a documented decision by the HR owner, not a quiet drift.

**Accountability.** Name three roles at kickoff: an HR policy owner (owns reference answers, adjudicates conflicts, signs off on the bar), a builder (owns pipeline, prompts, evaluation harness), and an operational owner post-launch (owns the review queue and the rollback decision). Ambiguity here is the most common reason pilots stall at the go-live gate.

**Fabrication control.** Layered, because no single control is sufficient: answers must carry citations; abstention when coverage is thin; a display convention that makes the source and effective date visible so employees can verify; and a standing disclaimer that the assistant summarises policy and HR remains the authority for decisions. These reduce and surface fabrication risk — they do not eliminate it, which is why sampled review remains in place.

**Sensitive data.** Salary bands and individual HR records are excluded at ingestion (see pipeline). Additionally: no user query is answered from personal records; queries that appear to seek individual HR data receive a referral response. Query logs contain employee questions and should be treated as internal-confidential with a defined retention period agreed with HR and IT.

**Access.** Internal-only. If some policies are restricted to certain populations, retrieval must filter on the requester's entitlements — resolve this during the corpus audit, because retrofitting per-user filtering after indexing is expensive.

**Rollback trigger, pre-committed before launch.** Pause the assistant and revert to the existing HR/IT channel if either fires: (a) any confirmed fabricated policy claim reaching an employee, or (b) sampled live accuracy falling below the agreed bar over a defined review window. The exact window and sample size are set with HR at the Phase 3 gate. The rollback is a documented switch someone is on the hook to throw, not a hypothetical.

**Change control.** Prompts, chunking parameters and retrieval configuration are versioned like code. Every candidate change re-runs the golden set before promotion, so a metric movement is attributable.

### Delivery approach — thin core loop, measured early

Constraint: one builder, one quarter; the engagement states 8 weeks on a fixed fee. All durations below are **estimates** and should be re-baselined after the corpus audit, which is the step most likely to change the shape of the work.

**Sequencing principle:** build the thinnest RAG loop that can be scored against the acceptance bar, score it, then improve only what the failure taxonomy says is broken. Resist scope — no chat memory, no personalisation, no action-taking in the pilot.

**Week-by-week shape (estimate):** corpus audit and golden-set drafting run in parallel up front, because HR's time is the scarce resource and reference answers take longer to agree than they appear to. Ingestion and a baseline hybrid retrieval loop follow. First full golden-set run should happen early enough that there is real time to fix retrieval misses — aim for a scored baseline by roughly the midpoint rather than the final week.

**Gates.** Nothing reaches employees before the golden set clears the bar offline. Then shadow mode (HR answers as usual; assistant answers are logged and compared silently), then limited release to a defined group with sampled review, then broader rollout only if the bar holds on live traffic.

**Value tracking.** The ≈30 staff-hours/week figure is the team's own unaudited estimate. Before launch, capture a simple baseline — a two-week tally of repeat questions by category — so post-launch deflection can be compared against something real. Without this, the pilot's value claim will be unfalsifiable at renewal time.

**Handover.** Deliverables include the ingestion pipeline as code, the versioned golden set and evaluation harness, the failure-taxonomy log, the rollback runbook, and a short operating guide for the review queue owner.

### Integration and adoption notes

**Surface placement drives adoption.** A standalone web app will underperform an entry point where employees already ask these questions — the internal chat tool or the intranet page they currently search. Choose one surface for the pilot and instrument it; two surfaces double the integration work and halve the signal.

**Connector realities.** Document-store access typically requires an app registration and permissioning decision from IT; start that request in week one, as approval lead time is a common schedule risk and is outside the builder's control.

**Escalation handoff.** Every abstention and every flagged answer should offer a one-click path to the existing HR/IT intake channel, carrying the original question. This makes abstention useful rather than a dead end, and it generates the gap list HR can use to fix documentation — a secondary benefit worth reporting.

**Citation rendering.** Link directly to the source document and, where the store supports it, the specific section. A citation an employee cannot open is not verifiable and will not satisfy HR reviewers on the rubric.

## Data flows

**Corpus ingestion and refresh:** Connector reads policy PDFs and handbook pages from the document store on a scheduled cadence → Sensitivity screen excludes salary-band and individual-HR-record content; exclusions logged for HR audit → Convert to text (OCR or exclude image-only files), strip boilerplate, preserve tables and clause numbering → Chunk on heading/clause boundaries so each chunk is a citable, self-contained rule → Attach metadata: title, owner, effective date, version, authority tier, sensitivity label → Embed and write to the vector index alongside a lexical index; propagate updates and deletions so superseded policies leave the corpus
**Answering an employee question:** Employee asks a question in the chosen surface → Hybrid retrieval (dense + lexical) with metadata filters returns candidate chunks; reranking narrows to the strongest passages → Coverage/confidence check: if insufficient, abstain and offer a one-click handoff to HR/IT with the original question → Otherwise compose the answer strictly from retrieved passages, with citation to document, section and effective date → Answer streamed to the employee with source links and the HR-is-authoritative note → Query, retrieved chunks, answer and any user flag written to the evaluation store
**Evaluation and review loop:** Golden set (100 HR-owned items, including unanswerable and sensitive-probe items) runs against every candidate configuration change → HR reviewers score with the rubric: correctness, citation validity, no unsupported claims, appropriate abstention → Fabrication count tracked as a separate hard gate alongside the correct-with-citation percentage → Failures classified as retrieval miss, synthesis error, citation error, stale source, or over-refusal → Fixes applied to the responsible layer; golden set re-run; version recorded → Sampled live answers and user flags scored on the same rubric; new failure modes and red-team findings promoted into the golden set

## Milestones

- **Phase 0 — Lock the bar and the corpus reality** — Confirm the acceptance bar with the HR owner, complete a hands-on audit of the policy corpus, and draft the 100-question golden set from real question history. _(~2 weeks (estimate))_. Exit: HR owner has signed the acceptance bar verbatim; corpus inventory lists every candidate document with owner, effective date and authority status; conflict-adjudication rule agreed; sensitive-content exclusion boundary defined in writing; golden set drafted with HR-authored reference answers and citations, including unanswerable and sensitive-probe items.. Risk owner: HR policy owner (reference answers, conflict rulings) with builder support.
- **Phase 1 — Ingestion pipeline and thin retrieval loop** — Stand up repeatable ingestion with metadata and sensitivity exclusion, plus the thinnest hybrid-retrieval answer loop capable of producing a cited answer or an abstention. _(~2–3 weeks (estimate))_. Exit: Pipeline runs end-to-end from source to index as versioned code with no manual steps; excluded content verifiably absent from the index; the loop returns a cited answer or an explicit abstention for every golden-set item; a first full golden-set run is scored and failures are classified by taxonomy.. Risk owner: Builder.
- **Phase 2 — Iterate to the offline bar** — Close the gap to the acceptance bar by fixing the specific failure categories the taxonomy identifies, and run targeted red-teaming on confident-but-fabricated answers. _(~2 weeks (estimate))_. Exit: Golden-set run meets ≥90% correct-with-citation as rated by HR reviewers AND records zero fabricated policy claims; red-team findings added to the golden set and re-run clean; if LLM-as-judge is used, its agreement with human ratings has been measured on a subsample and documented.. Risk owner: Builder, with HR reviewers scoring.
- **Phase 3 — Shadow mode and rollback commitment** — Run the assistant silently alongside the existing HR/IT channel, comparing its answers to what humans actually send, and pre-commit the launch and rollback conditions. _(~1–2 weeks (estimate))_. Exit: Shadow comparisons on live questions hold the acceptance bar on a sampled review; rollback trigger (metric, threshold, review window) documented and signed by the operational owner; baseline tally of repeat-question volume captured for later value comparison.. Risk owner: Operational owner.
- **Phase 4 — Limited release with sampled review** — Release to a defined employee group on the chosen surface, with spot-check review of sampled answers and a working escalation handoff. _(~2 weeks (estimate))_. Exit: Sampled live answers hold ≥90% correct-with-citation with zero confirmed fabrications over the agreed review window; escalation and flag paths demonstrably working; abstention rate reviewed and judged acceptable rather than masking coverage gaps.. Risk owner: Operational owner with HR reviewers.
- **Phase 5 — Broader rollout and handover** — Extend access, hand over the review queue and runbooks, and report measured outcomes against the pre-launch baseline. _(~1 week (estimate))_. Exit: Acceptance bar sustained on live sampled review post-expansion; review queue owner operating independently using the handover guide; documentation-gap list from abstentions delivered to HR; deflection reported against the pre-launch repeat-question baseline with the estimate's limitations stated.. Risk owner: Operational owner.

## Independent critic audit

**Critic verdict:** SHIP WITH FIXES

**Load-bearing gaps**
- **No no-go / kill path if Phase 2 cannot reach the bar** — Every phase gate assumes forward motion ('Close the gap to the acceptance bar'). There is no defined decision point for what happens if the golden-set run stalls at, say, 78% correct-with-citation after the taxonomy fixes are exhausted — who decides to descope, extend, or stop the pilot, and against what date. Given the 8-week engagement is shorter than the estimated phase sum, this is the most likely branch and it is unwritten.
- **'Zero fabricated policy claims' is treated as verifiable when the design only samples** — The bar says zero fabrication tolerated, and Phase 4 exits on 'zero confirmed fabrications over the agreed review window' — but production review is spot-check sampling, so the plan can only ever demonstrate zero fabrications in the sample. The plan never states this statistical limit or defines the sampling rate/power needed to make the claim meaningful, nor the confidence with which a 100-item golden set can support a zero-tolerance clause.
- **Verification of sensitive-content exclusion is asserted, not specified** — Phase 1 exits on 'excluded content verifiably absent from the index' but no test procedure exists — no adversarial index query suite, no reconciliation of the exclusion log against index contents, no owner for the check. Compliance ('must not expose salary bands or individual HR records') hinges entirely on this unspecified verification.
- **No rollback mechanics for the index itself** — The rollback trigger covers pausing the assistant, but there is no plan for reverting a bad ingestion run (e.g. a refresh that deletes live policies or ingests a superseded version). Index versioning/snapshotting is absent from both the architecture and the ingestion data flow, despite 'propagate updates and deletions' being called out as safety-critical.

**Top fixes**
- Resolve the 8-week vs ~10–12-week schedule contradiction: name which phases are compressible, what gets descoped first, and the date at which the sponsor is told the bar cannot be met in the fixed-fee window. Add an explicit no-go branch for Phase 2 failing the offline bar.
- Strip or label the unsupported quantitative and superlative rhetoric: 'double the integration work and halve the signal' (remove), 'the single most common cause of confidently-cited wrong answers', 'the most common reason pilots stall at the go-live gate', 'near-zero cost to coverage' (label as the builder's judgement, not fact).
- Specify the verification procedure and owner for sensitive-content exclusion (adversarial index probes plus exclusion-log-to-index reconciliation) before Phase 1 can claim 'verifiably absent', and state the sampling rate and its statistical limits behind the 'zero fabrications' live claim.

**Overclaims flagged**
- "Otherwise compose the answer strictly from retrieved passages" and "answers must be assembled from retrieved passages rather than model memory" — RAG constrains but does not enforce this; nothing in the design mechanically prevents the generator drawing on parametric knowledge, which is exactly the fabrication mode the bar targets.
- "excluded content verifiably absent from the index" — no verification method is defined, so 'verifiably' promises more than the design supports.
- "Pipeline runs end-to-end from source to index as versioned code with no manual steps" is asserted as a Phase 1 exit while the pipeline also includes 'a manual review of anything flagged' by the sensitivity screen — the exclusion path is human-gated by design.
- "it removes a whole class of fabrication risk at near-zero cost to coverage" — coverage cost of dropping FAQ threads is unmeasured at plan time.

**Fabrication scan**
- `two surfaces double the integration work and halve the signal` → must-remove
- `Expect duplicates, superseded drafts and "final_v3_FINAL" collisions — these are the single most common cause of confidently-cited wrong answers.` → must-label
- `Ambiguity here is the most common reason pilots stall at the go-live gate.` → must-label
- `it removes a whole class of fabrication risk at near-zero cost to coverage` → must-label
- `Data readiness scored 3 out of 5, the lowest input in the evaluation.` → ok
- `A managed vector index plus lexical retrieval` → ok

**Consistency issues**
- Milestone durations sum to ~10–12 weeks (2 + 2–3 + 2 + 1–2 + 2 + 1) against an engagement that 'states 8 weeks on a fixed fee'. The assumptions acknowledge the conflict ('tighter than the sum of the estimated phases') but no phase is marked descopable and no trigger for cutting scope exists — the plan flags the contradiction instead of resolving it.
- The executive summary says delivery is 'scoped to one builder over roughly one quarter (the engagement states 8 weeks...)', conflating the grounding's budget note ('one builder, one quarter') with the 8-week engagement without picking one planning horizon.
- Phase 3's exit criterion requires shadow comparisons 'to what humans actually send', but no data flow or architecture component captures the human HR/IT answer for comparison — the 'Answering an employee question' flow ends at the evaluation store with no human-answer join.
- Governance mentions per-user entitlement filtering ('retrieval must filter on the requester's entitlements') while assumptions say no entitlement filtering is needed; the architecture and ingestion flow contain no identity/entitlement component, so the governance commitment is unbacked.

**Acceptance bar is the spine:** yes — The bar is quoted verbatim in the evaluation section and is the literal exit criterion of Phases 2, 4 and 5 ('meets ≥90% correct-with-citation as rated by HR reviewers AND records zero fabricated policy claims'), with fabrication tracked as a separate hard gate.
